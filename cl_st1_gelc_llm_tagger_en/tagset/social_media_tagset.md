Social Media / Digital Language Tagset

A. Abbreviations and initialisms

Grouped by written form. The function of each is stated in the entry.

1. AF (as fuck)

A postpositive intensifier meaning "extremely" or "to a very high degree."

That movie was boring AF.
She looks confident af.

2. AITA (Am I the Asshole?)

A conventionalized question format used to present an interpersonal conflict
and ask others to judge the speaker's behavior.

AITA for refusing to lend my friend money?
AITA if I don't invite him to the wedding?

3. DM (direct message)

A private message sent through a digital platform. Also a verb.

Send me a DM with the details.
She DMed me after seeing the post.

Compare: DM as Dungeon Master in tabletop contexts.

4. FMC (female main character)

The female main character of a book or story. Widely used in online reading communities, particularly BookTok, Bookstagram, romance, and fan communities.

I loved the FMC, but I didn't care about the romance.
Looking for a fantasy book with a morally complex FMC.

5. FOMO (fear of missing out)

Anxiety or uneasiness associated with the possibility of missing an experience, event, opportunity, or social interaction.

I only went to the party because of FOMO.
Seeing everyone's vacation photos gave me serious FOMO.

6. FR (for real)

Used to signal sincerity, emphasis, or strong agreement.

That was the best episode, fr.
This class is impossible. Fr.

7. FYP (For You Page)

The personalized recommendation feed on TikTok and similar platforms.

This video showed up on my FYP.
My FYP is suddenly full of cooking videos.

Note: also used as a hashtag with no referential content (#fyp), posted to
solicit distribution rather than to refer to the feed.

8. GRWM (get ready with me)

A content format in which someone records or describes the process of getting
ready.

GRWM: Date night makeup and outfit check.
GRWM for tonight's birthday party!

9. GTFO (get the fuck out)

A vulgar initialism used as a directive to leave or dismiss someone, and also as a reaction expressing disbelief or surprise.

If you're going to insult everyone, GTFO.
GTFO! Is that test score even real?

10. IYKYK (if you know, you know)

Signals that a reference, joke, experience, or meaning is primarily accessible to people with relevant background knowledge.

That little café behind the station — IYKYK.
IYKYK. I'm not explaining what happened at the party.

11. LMAO (laughing my ass off)

Expresses amusement. A stronger version of LOL. Extended spellings such as LMFAO and LMAOO are intensity variants of the same item.

LMAO, that ending was ridiculous.
You really posted that publicly? LMAO.

12. LOL (laughing out loud)

Expresses amusement, and also functions as a discourse particle that softens or frames a statement without implying laughter. The second use is the more frequent one in current usage and is easy to over-tag as the first.

LOL, I can't believe you actually did that. → amusement
I thought you were serious lol. → softening particle

13. MMC (male main character)

The male main character of a book or story, often the primary male romantic
lead. Used in the same communities as FMC.

The MMC was my favorite character in the entire series.
I need a romance with a grumpy MMC and a confident FMC.

14. NGL (not gonna lie)

Used to introduce a candid or sincere opinion, often one that goes against the
speaker's expected position.

NGL, I actually liked the remake.
The ending was disappointing, ngl.

15. NPC (non-player character)

Originally a game character controlled by the system rather than a player. Figuratively, a person perceived as mechanical, predictable, passive, or lacking independent thought.

Talk to the NPC to start the quest. → gaming sense
He's acting like an NPC. → figurative sense
Everyone in the waiting room had total NPC energy.

Note: the figurative sense is pejorative and occurs both in light humor and in
dehumanizing political rhetoric. 

16. NPD (Narcissistic Personality Disorder)

In social-media discourse the abbreviation is frequently used non-clinically, as an informal label applied to an ex-partner, a parent, or a public figure, generally by someone with no diagnostic standing and about a person who has not been assessed.

Every one of his exes says he has NPD.
Classic NPD behavior, he can't take any criticism at all.

17. OMG (Oh My God)

Expresses surprise, excitement, shock, or disbelief.

OMG, this looks amazing!
"You actually met her?" "OMG, yes."

18. OOMF (one of my friends / one of my followers)

Used to refer indirectly to a friend or follower without explicitly identifying that person, often when the referent is expected to recognize themselves.

OOMF really needs to find a hobby instead of worrying about what I post.
Not OOMF watching all my stories but never liking or replying.

19. OOTD (outfit of the day)

A post, photograph, or video presenting someone's outfit.

Sunday brunch OOTD. Kept it simple and comfy!
Matching my coffee to my OOTD.

Compare: OTD (21) — outfit of the day is written OOTD, never OTD.

20. OP (original poster / original post)

May refer either to the person who originally created a post or to the original post itself. Animate predicates favor the first reading; verbs of reading, editing, and linking favor the second.

OP explained what happened in an update. → original poster
I went back and reread the OP. → original post

Compare: OP as "overpowered" in gaming discourse — That build is so OP.

21. OTD (of the day / on this day)

Of the day is used in recurring daily content. On this day introduces a past event, anniversary, or memory associated with the current date, and is nearly always followed by a year or a date.

Recipe OTD: my grandmother's chocolate cake. → of the day
OTD in 2005, this album was released. → on this day

22. POV (point of view)

A framing device used to introduce a hypothetical, dramatized, or perspectivized situation, positioning the viewer inside it.

POV: You are opening the oven to check on a batch of fresh chocolate chip
cookies.
POV: You finally complete a task you put off for weeks and it only takes five
minutes.


23. QRT (quote retweet)

A repost of another user's content accompanied by additional commentary. Also a verb.

Her QRT got more attention than the original post.
People kept QRTing the announcement with jokes.

24. RN (right now)

A temporal expression referring to the present moment.

I can't talk rn.
What are you doing rn?

Compare: RN as Registered Nurse, frequent in bios and healthcare text.

25. SA (sexual assault)

Frequently used in content warnings and in discussions of sexual assault. Depending on context it may also function as algospeak, used to avoid explicit terminology or perceived moderation restrictions. See section H.

The video includes a content warning for SA.
Free and confidential support is available for victims of SA.
She used "SA" throughout the video because she believed the full term might
reduce its reach. → algospeak use

Compare: S.A. as a company suffix; SA as South Africa, South Australia, or San
Antonio.

26. SH (self-harm)

Frequently used in content warnings and in discussions of self-harm. As with SA, it may also function as algospeak where context indicates avoidance. See section H.

The caption includes a content warning for SH.
The content moderation team quickly removed the post because it promoted SH.

27. SMH (shaking my head)

Expresses disapproval, disbelief, frustration, or disappointment.

He posted the password publicly. SMH.
SMH, people are still sharing that fake story.

28. SYBAU (shut your bitch ass up)

A vulgar initialism functioning as a forceful or humorous directive telling someone to stop speaking. Frequently used jokingly between people on good terms.

SYBAU, it's a movie not a documentary, you weirdo.
Sybau, we're just trying to have fun.

29. TBH (to be honest)

Used to signal a frank or sincere opinion.

TBH, I wouldn't buy it again.
It wasn't worth the price, tbh.

30. W / L (win / loss)

Single-letter forms used to evaluate something positively or negatively. Also occur attributively (a W take, an L take) and in take the L.

Common-sense take. W.
Canceling the show after one season was an L.

B. Nouns

31. almond mom

A negative label for a mother or caregiver perceived as promoting restrictive eating, thinness, or unhealthy attitudes toward food and body image.

Growing up with an almond mom meant our fridge only had celery sticks instead of actual snacks.
His advice about carbs gave him total almond mom energy.

32. aura

Perceived charisma, coolness, presence, or social prestige, often humorously conceptualized as measurable and as something that can be gained, lost, or accumulated. The traditional sense of a distinctive atmosphere or quality remains in use and is separated by the quantifying predicates the slang sense takes: lose, gain, farm, plus and minus.

The room had an aura of mystery. → traditional sense
He lost all his aura after that comment.
Walking away without responding gave her serious aura.

33. aura points

Humorous imaginary units representing gains or losses in coolness, charisma, prestige, or social presence. Almost always with an explicit numeral.

Catching the phone without looking was plus 500 aura points.
How many aura points do I lose for tripping in front of everyone?

34. babygirl

Traditionally a term of endearment directed toward a girl or woman. In contemporary fandom slang it refers affectionately or humorously to an attractive male celebrity or fictional character, especially one perceived as cute, sensitive or vulnerable. 

Come here, baby girl. → traditional term of endearment
He is internet's favorite babygirl. 
That emotionally damaged villain is my babygirl.

35. bop

A derogatory noun for a person, particularly a young woman, perceived as sexually promiscuous. Animacy of the referent separates this from the older sense of a catchy song.

He started spreading rumors and calling her a bop just because she rejected him.
I hate how guys use the word bop to shame girls who just like to go out and party.

Compare: That song is a bop. → catchy or excellent song.

36. brainrot / brain rot

The perceived deterioration of attention, taste, or thinking caused by heavy consumption of repetitive, trivial, absurd, or low-quality internet content. Also the content itself, and the vocabulary and references that circulate with it.

Watching those clips all weekend gave me brainrot.
My entire feed is football brainrot right now.

37. canon event

A formative or apparently unavoidable experience regarded as necessary to someone's personal development, frequently used humorously. 

Getting that terrible haircut at sixteen was a canon event.
Don't warn him about that relationship. He needs this canon event.

38. cap

A lie or false statement. As a verb, to lie, exaggerate, or make a false claim. The slang noun is a mass noun and takes no article, which separates it from the ordinary senses.

That's cap.
Stop capping.
Why are you capping about how much you paid for it?

Compare: He put on a baseball cap. → ordinary meaning.

39. choppelganger

A portmanteau of chopped and doppelganger referring humorously or insultingly to a less attractive person who resembles someone else.

They look alike, but he's definitely the choppelganger.
Uncle Dave looks like Christian Bale's choppelganger.

40. clout

Influence, visibility, prestige, or social status, especially online. Also in clout-chasing and clout chaser.

He only posted that for clout.
Creators with enough clout can make a minor trend go viral.

41. coffee badging

The practice of briefly going to a physical workplace, often to register attendance and be seen, before leaving to work remotely elsewhere.

A lot of hybrid workers admitted to coffee badging.
He went into the office for an hour, did some coffee badging, and finished the day at home.

42. Duet

A TikTok feature that allows a creator to record a new video alongside another user's existing video. Also a verb.

Her Duet with the original creator went viral.
You should duet this video and show your reaction.

Compare: the musical sense. 

43. enshittification

The process by which digital platforms and services steadily become worse over time as their operators shift from serving users to extracting value from them. Also a verb, enshittify.

Users blame the platform's enshittification for the worsening search results.
The article traces the enshittification of the service after its business model changed.

44. Fanum tax

A humorous expression referring to taking part of another person's food as a "tax." Also a verb. From a streamer's name.

I took one fry as my fanum tax.
He fanum-taxed my pizza before I even sat down.

45. finsta

A secondary, usually private, or less publicly curated Instagram account.

She only posts those pictures on her finsta.
On my finsta, I am more open about my political opinions.

46. flop era

A period characterized by failure, disappointment, declining popularity, or things generally going badly, often used humorously or self-deprecatingly. The frame in one's X era is productive; see 129.

My posts are getting no engagement. I'm in my flop era.
Fans say the singer has finally escaped her flop era.

47. girl dinner

A humorous expression for a simple, low-effort meal composed of snacks, leftovers, or assembled components rather than a conventional cooked meal.

Cheese, crackers, olives, and fruit — girl dinner.
I don't feel like cooking, so girl dinner it is.

48. green flag

A positive sign indicating desirable, healthy, reassuring, or compatible qualities, especially in relationships. Back-formed from red flag.

Being able to apologize is a green flag.
He treats the staff well — major green flag.

49. the ick

A sudden feeling of aversion or loss of attraction toward a romantic interest,sometimes triggered by a relatively minor behavior. Nearly always with the definite article, in give someone the ick or get the ick.

The way he spoke to the waiter gave me the ick.
Once I get the ick, it's difficult to recover.

50. Karen

A pejorative internet label for a person, stereotypically a middle-aged whitewoman, perceived as entitled, demanding, excessively complaining, or attempting to exercise social authority over others.

She demanded to speak to the manager, clearly a Karen.
Don't go full Karen because they got your order wrong.
 
Compare: the ordinary given name. Evidence for the label: an indefinite article, plural marking, or a degree modifier. A bare capitalized Karen in subject position is usually just a name.

51. lore

The accumulated background, history, or contextual information surrounding a person, community, event, or recurring online situation. The traditional sense of traditional knowledge or stories takes a tradition or a fictional universe as its possessor, the internet sense takes a specific person or a small online community.

The book contains ancient lore. → traditional sense
What's the lore behind this influencer?
Fans love debating the complex lore of this book.

52. main character energy / main character syndrome

Main character energy describes a strong sense of self-confidence and charisma, and conspicuous behavior resembling that of the protagonist of a narrative — living as if you are the hero of your own story. Main character syndrome describes self-centered behavior in which someone appears to treat other people as secondary characters in their own narrative. The two are not interchangeable: energy is usually approving, syndrome consistently pejorative.

She walked into the job interview with main character energy.
Walking through the airport with that soundtrack gave her main character energy.
His main character syndrome makes him a terrible listener.
That kind of behavior is classic main character syndrome.

Note: X energy is a productive frame — chaotic energy, NPC energy, almond mom energy — and should be handled as a construction rather than as a set of listed items.

53. milkshake duck

A person or thing that becomes rapidly popular or beloved online and is then discovered to have an offensive, objectionable, or otherwise disappointing past or characteristic. Also a verb.

The internet found its new favorite creator, and two days later he became a milkshake duck.
The internet milkshake-ducked him within a day.

54. mutual / moot

A user with whom another user has a reciprocal following relationship. Moot is an informal clipping of mutual.

We've been mutuals for years but have never met offline.
One of my moots sent me the video.

Compare: mutual as an ordinary adjective (mutual respect); moot as an adjective
meaning debatable (a moot point). The slang item is a count noun, usually plural or possessed.

55. pick-me girl / pick me

A derogatory label for a woman perceived as seeking male approval by emphasizing that she is different from or superior to other women, often while disparaging them. Also attributive: pick-me behavior, pick-me energy.

Just got called a pick me girl because I said that I enjoy cooking for my boyfriend.
Stop being a pick-me and just admit that you like pop music like the rest of us.

56. rage bait / rage-bait

Content deliberately constructed to provoke anger or outrage in order to attract attention or engagement. Also a verb; related forms are rage-baiting, rage-baited, and rage-baiter.

That post isn't a serious opinion, it's rage bait.
He's rage-baiting his followers.

Note: distinct from rage farming (130), which names the sustained strategy rather than the individual artifact.

57. receipts

Evidence supporting a claim or accusation, especially screenshots, recordings, messages, or other digital records. Almost always plural, and collocating with bring, post, drop, and have.

If you're going to accuse him, bring the receipts.
You better have the receipts if you're posting something like this.

Compare: Keep the receipts from the store. → proofs of purchase.

58. red flag

A warning sign indicating potentially problematic, dangerous, unhealthy, or incompatible behavior or qualities.

Controlling who your partner talks to is a red flag.
That comment would be an immediate red flag for me.

59. reply guy

A habitual and often unwanted social-media replier whose responses may be overly familiar, persistent, flirtatious, condescending, or pedantic.

He turned into a classic reply guy, sliding into her comments with unwanted advice.
Please ignore the reply guys in the comments, they just want to argue about nothing.

60. rizz

Short for charisma. A person's charm and appeal, and their skill at attracting or flirting with others. Romantic appeal is the most frequent application but not the limit of the term. Also a verb, usually rizz up: to charm or win someone over.

He has absolutely no rizz.
Her awkward rizz somehow works every time.
How did you manage to rizz up the bartender that quickly?

61. shadowban / shadow ban

A restriction that reduces the visibility or reach of a user's account or content without making the restriction obvious to the affected user. Also a verb. Frequently a user's suspicion rather than a confirmed platform action.

Users blamed the drop in views on a shadowban.
I think the platform shadowbanned my account.

62. sigma

A person perceived as independent, self-assured, cool, or unconcerned with social approval, often used ironically. Occurs as a noun, attributively (a sigma move), and predicatively (that makes him sigma).

He's trying to be a sigma.
The whole sigma persona feels forced.
That was a surprisingly sigma move.

Compare: the Greek letter, and six sigma in management text.

63. simp

A person perceived as showing excessive admiration, attention, or devotion toward someone, typically without reciprocation. As a verb, to display strong or excessive admiration or devotion toward someone.

He is such a simp; he buys her expensive gifts when she barely texts him back.
Stop simping and just talk to her normally.
He tends to simp over every popular streamer on Twitch.

64. situationship

An ambiguous romantic or sexual relationship that lacks clearly defined status, commitment, or expectations.

They've been in a situationship for six months.
I thought we were dating, but apparently it was just a situationship.

65. slop / AI slop

Low-quality or low-effort, mass-produced digital content, especially material generated with
generative AI. The bare form is increasingly used in this sense without the AI modifier.

My feed is full of AI slop.
Half these images are just slop generated for engagement.

Compare: The animals were fed slop. → ordinary meaning.

66. stan

An extremely enthusiastic or devoted fan. Also a verb, to admire, support, or enthusiastically endorse someone or something. The negative reading survives in criticism of fandom behavior.

She's been a Beyoncé stan for years.
The stans flooded the comments after the announcement.
I stan her.
We've stanned this band since their first album.

67. Stitch

A TikTok feature that allows a creator to incorporate a clip from another user's video into their own, usually followed by a response, reaction, or commentary. Also a verb.

She made a Stitch responding to the original video.
I stitched his video to explain what he got wrong.

Compare: the ordinary sewing sense, and stitch together in general video editing. The platform sense takes a video or a creator as its object.

68. subtweet

A post indirectly referring to or criticizing someone without explicitly naming or tagging that person. Also a verb. No longer restricted to the platform its name comes from.

That post sounds like a subtweet.
She's clearly subtweeting her former roommate.

69. tea

Gossip, or revealing interpersonal information. A mass noun, collocating strongly with spill and with what's in questions.

Okay, what's the tea?
She finally spilled the tea about what happened backstage.

Compare: Would you like some tea? → the beverage.

70. tech bro

An informal, often disapproving label for a man working in the technology industry, stereotypically perceived as overconfident, socially unaware, or representative of "bro" culture. Part of a wider X bro pattern: crypto bro, finance bro, gym bro.

The coffee shop was packed with tech bros shouting buzzwords into their wireless earbuds.
The board replaced the CEO with a young tech bro who promised unrealistic AI growth.

71. thinspo / thinspiration

Online content centered on extreme thinness that may encourage harmful eating habits or drastic weight loss.

The platform restricted searches for thinspo content.
Researchers examined the circulation of thinspo images online.

Note: the term occurs most often in moderation policy, research, journalism, and recovery discourse, where it names the content rather than endorsing it. Presence of the term is not in itself evidence of promotion.

72. thirst trap

A photograph, video, or post intended to attract attention or desire.

He posted a thirst trap from the beach.
Her feed alternates between travel photos and thirst traps.

73. thread

A connected sequence of posts or comments functioning as a larger discourse unit.

Scroll down to the next part of this thread to see the results.
This thread will change the way you think about this.

Compare: the textile sense, and the concurrency sense in computing, which is frequent in the same technical registers.

74. tradwife

Short for traditional wife. A woman who presents or promotes a lifestyle centered on traditional homemaking, childcare, and gender roles. Used both as a self-description and as an outside label, with markedly different evaluative force in each case.

I post tradwife content about homemaking and family life.
Tradwives think the patriarchy is a healthy and necessary aspect of society.

75. unc

A clipping of uncle, used for someone perceived as old, aging, outdated, or behaving older than their age. Teasing rather than hostile, and also used as an address term and in unc status.

Look at him wearing those running shoes with jeans, he has officially reached unc status.
Stop trying to explain modern music to us, unc, we know what a playlist is.

76. zaddy

An attractive, often older, stylish or confident man. Never kinship, unlike daddy.

Pedro Pascal is the ultimate zaddy of the internet right now.
The entire comment section turned into a zaddy appreciation club.

77. zoomies

A sudden burst of energetic, frenetic activity, especially in pets, also humorously extended to humans and to tasks. Almost always the zoomies or X zoomies.

The dog gets the zoomies every night.
I got the cleaning zoomies and reorganized the entire apartment.

C. Verbs

78. cancel

To publicly withdraw support from a person, organization, brand, or other entity, typically because of behavior or statements regarded as objectionable.
Related: cancel culture, canceled.

People started trying to cancel him after the old posts resurfaced.
The internet canceled the brand over its response to the controversy.

Compare: They canceled the meeting. → ordinary meaning. The slang sense takes an animate or institutional object, the ordinary sense takes an event or a subscription.

79. crash out / crashout

To lose emotional control, becoming intensely angry, distressed, reckless, or overwhelmed. The noun crashout refers either to an episode of such behavior or to a person who behaves that way.

I'm going to crash out if they cancel the flight again.
He completely crashed out in the comments.
That argument caused a full crashout.
Don't become a crashout over one bad reply.

Compare: I crashed out on the couch. → fell asleep. Also crash out of a tournament in sports reporting.

80. doomscroll / doomscrolling

To consume a prolonged stream of distressing or negative online information, often compulsively. Also a noun.

I spent an hour doomscrolling instead of going to sleep.
Doomscrolling before bed makes it harder for me to switch off.
My nightly doomscroll is getting ridiculous.

81. dox / doxx

To publicly reveal or publish private or identifying information about someone, especially as a form of punishment, retaliation, intimidation, or harassment.
Related forms include doxed/doxxed and doxing/doxxing, with doxing also functioning as a noun.

Someone threatened to dox her after the argument.
The account was suspended after doxxing another user.
The victim of the doxing reported the harassment to the police.

82. eat / ate

To perform, present oneself, or execute something exceptionally well. Usually intransitive or with a performance-denoting object, very often in the past tense with an intensifying adverb.

She ate in the finale.
They absolutely ate that performance up.

Compare: the ordinary sense, which takes a food object. An animate subject with no object and a preceding absolutely, really, or totally is strong evidence for the slang reading. See also 116.

83. flex

To show off or conspicuously display or boast about possessions, status, achievements, appearance, or another desirable attribute. Also a noun.

He's flexing his new car on Instagram.
She keeps flexing her follower count.
That car is such a flex.
I just paid off all my student loans. It's my flex for the year.

Compare: He flexed his arm to show the muscle. → ordinary meaning.

84. gatekeep / gatekeeping

To deliberately withhold access to information, sources, recommendations, products, communities, or other resources, often when other users are explicitly asking for them. The person noun is gatekeeper.

Why are you gatekeeping this brand?
Stop trying to gatekeep the fandom.
The gatekeeping in these comments is ridiculous.
She's such a gatekeeper about underground bands.

Compare: the established sociological and journalistic sense of controlling institutional access, which is older and different.

85. ghost

To suddenly stop communicating with someone without explanation. Has spread well beyond dating to hiring, freelancing, and friendship. The noun ghosting names the practice.

She ghosted him after their second date.
Employer ghosting has become a frustrating problem for many job seekers.

Compare: The story is about a ghost. → the supernatural entity.

86. glaze

To praise, defend, promote, or admire someone excessively or uncritically. The person noun is glazer: a person who praises, supports, or admires someone to an excessive or embarrassing degree.

Stop glazing him, he played one good game.
The comments were glazing her before the video even started.
His biggest glazers are already defending the album.

Compare: She glazed the cake. → ordinary meaning.

87. glow down

A perceived negative transformation or decline in appearance, style, confidence, or overall presentation; formed as the opposite of glow up. The verb use is the more innovative one and remains less established than the noun.

That haircut was a glow-down.
I somehow glowed down over the summer.
People were arguing about whether the redesign was a glow-up or a glow-down.

88. glow up

A significant positive transformation in appearance, style, confidence, status, or another valued quality. Hyphenated as a noun more often than not; open as a verb.

She had a huge glow-up after college.
He really glowed up over the last few years.

89. hard launch

To explicitly make a relationship or a person publicly visible. Also a noun. Paired antonym of soft launch.

That picture was their hard launch.
They hard-launched the relationship yesterday on Instagram.

90. lock in

To enter a state of intense concentration, commitment, or focused effort. Intransitive with a human subject, unlike the ordinary transitive senses.

I need to lock in and finish this paper tonight.
Everyone lock in, the exam starts in ten minutes.

Compare: We locked in the mortgage rate. → ordinary meaning.

91. mog

To conspicuously outclass another person, especially in appearance, attractiveness, physique, status, or performance. Related forms include mogged, mogging, and mogger. From looksmaxxing and adjacent online subcultures.

He somehow mogged everyone in that photo.
She's mogging the entire cast on the red carpet.

92. ratio

As a noun, a disproportionately negative engagement relationship on a post. As a verb, to give a social-media post considerably more negative than positive responses, or in passive use, for a post to receive such a response. The passive is by far the most frequent form. Also used as a standalone one-word reply.

Users ratioed the post within an hour.
That announcement got completely ratioed.

Compare: The ratio is three to one. → ordinary meaning.

93. serve

To present an especially impressive appearance, style, attitude, or performance. Intransitive, or with a bare nominal complement taking no determiner.

She served all night in that outfit.
That photo is serving serious 1990s supermodel energy.

Compare: the ordinary senses of serving food, serving a sentence, or serving in tennis, none of which allows the bare determinerless complement.

94. ship

To support, imagine, or desire a romantic relationship between two people or characters. A clipping of relationship. Takes a pair as its object, often coordinated or a plural pronoun. Related: shipper, shipping, and the noun ship for the pairing itself.

I ship them.
Fans have been shipping those characters for years.
Those two are my favorite ship.

Compare: The ship arrived at port. → a vessel. The order shipped yesterday. → logistics.

95. slay

To perform, look, or succeed exceptionally well. Used adjective-like, it describes a person, outfit, appearance, performance, or other object of evaluation as excellent, impressive, or stylish. As an interjection, it is a positive reaction expressing strong approval, celebration, or encouragement. Also a noun.

She absolutely slayed in the final round.
They slayed that performance.
That outfit is slay.
"I passed!" "Slay!"

96. soft launch

To indirectly hint at a new relationship or person without fully identifying or revealing them. Also a noun. Paired antonym of hard launch.

That photo of two coffee cups was clearly a soft launch.
She soft-launched her boyfriend by posting only his hand.

97. yap

To talk excessively, ramble, or continue discussing something at length, often with humorous evaluation. Frequently self-directed and affectionate rather than critical. The person noun is yapper.

Why is he still yapping about this?
I could yap about this movie for hours.
I'm a professional yapper whenever someone mentions linguistics.

D. Adjectives

Most items in this section occur predicatively far more often than attributively, and do not inflect for degree.

98. based

Used approvingly for an opinion, action, or attitude perceived as bold, unapologetic, authentic, or correct. Also occurs as a standalone reply.

That take is surprisingly based.
A genuinely based response for once.

Compare: this is the most ambiguous item in the section. Based on and based in are far more frequent in general text. Evidence for the slang adjective: no following preposition, a degree modifier, or standalone use as a full turn.

99. boujee / bougie

Fancy, luxurious, high-class, or associated with wealth and indulgence. Depending on context, complimentary or mocking. From bourgeois.

This restaurant is way too boujee for me.
She turned a simple picnic into a boujee event.

100. bussin

Extremely good or enjoyable, especially but not exclusively when describing food. Intensified as bussin bussin.

This pasta is bussin.
They make bussin tacos at that place.

101. cheugy

Unfashionable, outdated, uncool, or trying too hard, especially in relation to trends associated stereotypically with millennials. Frequency is now declining and the term is often used self-consciously.

Apparently that décor is cheugy now.
I don't care if it's cheugy, I still like it.

102. chronically online

Describes a person, perspective, or behavior perceived as excessively shaped by internet culture and niche online discourse. Predicated of arguments and takes as readily as of people.

That argument is incredibly chronically online.
Only someone chronically online would recognize all those references.

103. cooked

Doomed, defeated, exhausted, in serious trouble, or beyond recovery. Takes an animate subject.

If the professor checks the citations, we're cooked.
After three nights without enough sleep, I am completely cooked.

Compare: The vegetables are cooked. → prepared by cooking.

104. cringe

Embarrassing, awkward, or likely to cause second-hand embarrassment. The bare adjectival use is the innovation, the verb and the noun of the physical reaction are older. Also cringey / cringy.

That video is painfully cringe.
I cringe whenever I see it.
The awkward silence after his bad joke created a collective cringe in the room.

105. delulu

Delusional or unrealistically hopeful; also used playfully for extreme optimism or self-confidence. A reduplicated clipping of delusional, usually self-applied and affectionate, which distinguishes it from the base word.

She's being delulu if she thinks he'll text back.
Maybe I'm delulu, but I think we can win.

106. fire

Excellent, impressive, exciting, or highly appealing.

That track is fire.
She showed up in a fire outfit.

Compare: The fire spread through the building. → combustion. The noun is vastly more frequent overall, so this reading needs positive evidence: an evaluable subject such as song, outfit, take, or meal, in copular syntax.

107. goated

Exceptionally skilled, legendary, or the best, from greatest of all time. Also in the fixed intensifier goated with the sauce.

That player is goated.
This is one of the most goated episodes in the series.

Note: the related noun is GOAT, normally capitalized — Many fans believe that
athlete is the ultimate GOAT — and is a separate item.

Compare: goat as the animal, and the older sense of the person blamed for a loss, which is the opposite evaluation.

108. mid

Mediocre, disappointing, underwhelming, or not particularly good.

The movie was mid.
People waited three years for a mid sequel.

Compare: the ordinary sense of middle (mid-season, mid-nineties, mid-range), which is far more frequent and normally hyphenated or bound. Free-standing predicative mid with an evaluable subject is the slang item.

109. neurospicy

A playful, nontechnical slang adjective used by some speakers as an informal alternative to neurodivergent. Overwhelmingly self-applied, and not a clinical term.

She describes herself online as neurospicy.
The group posts neurospicy memes about everyday experiences.

110. parasocial

Describes a one-sided perceived relationship or sense of intimacy with a public figure, creator, fictional character, AI system, or other mediated entity. A term from media research that has passed into general online usage, where it is often evaluative rather than descriptive.

Some followers develop parasocial relationships with influencers.
That level of parasocial attachment can make ordinary criticism feel personal.

111. sus

Suspicious, questionable, or seemingly untrustworthy. A clipping of suspicious, boosted by a party game.

That link looks sus.
There was some sus behavior in the comments.

Compare: sus as an abbreviation of suspended or sustained in technical text. sus4 in chord notation.

112. terminally online

An emphatic variant of chronically online, and more critical in force, since chronically online can be self-deprecating.

This discourse feels terminally online.
He's become way too terminally online.

113. thicc

Attractively full-figured or thickset. Used to compliment curvy women or brawny men, and often humorously applied to husky animals or hefty objects. The respelling is what marks the evaluative sense; ordinary thick does not carry it.

That character is surprisingly thicc.
Look at those thicc little cat paws.

114. thirsty

Excessively eager for romantic or sexual attention, approval, or attention more generally. Predicated of comments, replies, and posts as well as of people.

The replies under that photo are incredibly thirsty.
Don't sound so thirsty in the comments.

--------
E. Interjections and discourse markers

Initialisms that function as interjections or markers — OMG, LOL, LMAO, SMH,
FR, NGL, TBH, IYKYK — are in section A, grouped there by written form. The
interjection use of slay is at 95.

115. bet

Signals acceptance, agreement, acknowledgment, or readiness to act. Occurs as a full turn or clause-initially, and never takes an object or a determiner.

"I'll send you the file tonight." "Bet."
Bet, I'll take care of it.

Compare: I lost the bet. → the noun of wagering.

116. gyatt / gyat

A slang exclamation derived from "goddamn," used to express shock, surprise, excitement, or admiration — frequently when seeing an attractively curvy person or a large backside, but also used more broadly for something impressive. The derived noun refers to a large backside.

She walked into the party in that outfit, and everyone was like, Gyatt!
Gyat, that car going down the street looks amazing!
That trick shot was gyat level insane!
He has a gyatt.

117. highkey

Signals a relatively strong, overt, or unhidden stance or degree. Back-formed from lowkey and less frequent than it.

Highkey, that was the best part of the movie.
I'm highkey nervous about tomorrow.

118. lowkey

Downtones or backgrounds a stance, feeling, desire, or evaluation. Often does not genuinely reduce commitment; it hedges the social risk of the statement rather than its strength.

Lowkey, I want to leave early.
I'm lowkey obsessed with this song.

Compare: a low-key ceremony. → the ordinary adjective, normally hyphenated and attributive.

119. no cap

Signals sincerity or truthfulness and rejects exaggeration or deception. The negation of cap (38).

That's the best concert I've ever seen, no cap.
No cap, I thought we were going to lose.

120. six seven / 6-7 / 67

A memetic numerical expression functioning as an interjection or in-group signal without stable propositional meaning. Typically triggered by any occurrence of the numbers in surrounding speech.

Someone said he was six foot seven and the whole class yelled "SIX SEVEN!"
67! 67!

Compare: There were 67 participants. → ordinary numeral. The memetic use is exclamatory, repeated, or chorused, and has no quantificational role.

121. skibidi

A highly flexible meme term whose interpretation varies substantially according to context and is frequently absent altogether. Occurs in adjective, noun, and slot-filler positions. The model should not force a single stable meaning onto the form; positional flexibility is the diagnostic property, not a sign that several senses need to be distinguished.

That's so skibidi.
What in the skibidi is happening here?

122. womp womp

A mocking, dismissive, or playful reaction to disappointment, failure, complaint, or misfortune. Dismissive of the complaint rather than sympathetic, which distinguishes it from ordinary commiseration.

"My team lost again." "Womp womp."
You missed the deadline? Womp womp, that's on you.

--------
F. Fixed expressions

Formulas and constructions, including those with open slots, written X. Ordinary compound nouns that happen to be multiword — thirst trap, reply guy, green flag — are listed under their class instead.

123. ate and left no crumbs

Strongly positive expression indicating that someone performed or executed something exceptionally well and completely. An extension of eat / ate (82).

She ate and left no crumbs.
They absolutely ate and left no crumbs with that performance.

124. choose violence / I choose violence

Indicates a decision to be deliberately confrontational, provocative, audacious, or disruptive rather than diplomatic. Figurative, the literal reading is not intended.

She woke up and chose violence in the group chat.
Posting that ranking today? You really chose violence.

125. it's giving X

Indicates that someone or something evokes, resembles, projects, or creates a particular impression, identity, aesthetic, or vibe. Also occurs without a complement, as an evaluative use indicating whether something produces the desired or expected effect. Only the complementless form has a negative variant.

It's giving rich aunt.
This lighting is giving horror movie.
That dress is giving.
The makeup tonight is not giving.

126. I was today years old

Humorous formula indicating that the speaker has only just discovered something. Almost always followed by when.

I was today years old when I learned you can do this on your phone.
I was today years old when someone told me what that icon means.

127. is X in the room with us?

Sarcastic construction used to challenge a claim that some quality, phenomenon, or evidence is present. The slot normally repeats the abstract noun from the preceding claim, with a definite article.

Their chemistry is incredible. Is the chemistry in the room with us?
You keep talking about the improvement. Is the improvement in the room with us?

128. let X cook

Means that someone should be allowed to continue developing an idea, argument, plan, or performance before being interrupted or judged. The slot is normally a pronoun or a name.

I don't know where he's going with this, but let him cook.
Let her cook — the presentation isn't finished yet.

129. live rent-free / rent-free in someone's head

Describes something that remains persistently or disproportionately present in someone's thoughts. Also reduced to rent-free as a predicate modifier. Usually accusatory in the second person and self-deprecating in the first.

That song has been living rent-free in my head all week.
Why is one random comment still living rent-free in your head?

Compare: She lived in the apartment rent-free. → ordinary meaning.

130. the saxophones are getting louder

Signals that warning signs are accumulating and an unpleasant or inevitable outcome appears to be approaching. From a piece of music used to signal impending doom in video edits.

I checked my bank balance after the trip and the saxophones are getting louder.
The professor just said, "See me after class." The saxophones are getting louder.

131. stitch incoming

A framing expression displayed as a caption or on-screen text at the beginning of a Stitch video. It signals that the initial clip comes from another creator and that the current creator's response, reaction, critique, or commentary will follow. Occurs in captions and overlays, effectively never in running prose.

STITCH INCOMING
  [The original creator's clip plays first, followed by the current creator's
  rebuttal.]

stitch incoming 👀
  [On-screen text appears while a controversial original clip plays, before the
  creator responds.]

132. touch grass

A directive telling someone to spend less time online, regain perspective, or reconnect with offline life.

You've been arguing with strangers for six hours. Touch grass.
He told me to touch grass after I wrote ten paragraphs about the game.

133. understood the assignment

Indicates that someone understood what was expected stylistically, creatively, or performatively and executed it particularly well. Almost always past tense, third person, with no overt assignment in the discourse.

She understood the assignment.
The costume department understood the assignment with this one.

Compare: the literal educational sense, frequent in school and workplace contexts.

--------
G. Productive formations

Sub-lexical patterns. Lexicalized instances are listed inside the entry rather than given separate numbers. The model should generalize to novel formations following each pattern.

134. -coded

Indicates that something evokes, resembles, or is implicitly associated with an identity, character type, aesthetic, genre, institution, or cultural category. Examples include villain-coded, Disney-coded, corporate-coded, protagonist-coded, startup-coded, etc . Hyphenation is inconsistent.

That monologue is extremely villain-coded.
The entire office is startup-coded.

135. -core

Creates labels for aesthetics, styles, subcultures, or identity-associated clusters. Examples include cottagecore, Barbiecore, normcore, cluttercore, etc . Written closed by default.

Her apartment is pure cottagecore.
Fall's trendiest look relies on normcore white sneakers and loose-fitting denim.

Compare: the music-genre words from which the suffix was extracted (hardcore, metalcore) are established lexemes, not instances of this pattern.

136. in one's X era

Frames a current phase of life or behavior as a defined period with a characteristic theme. The slot takes a noun or an adjective. Often self-applied and humorous. Examples include flop era (46), villain era, healing era.

I'm in my flop era.
She's in her healing era.
He's fully in his villain era.

137. -maxxing

Indicates intensive optimization, maximization, adoption, or exaggerated pursuit of the property or activity represented by the base. Examples include looksmaxxing, sleepmaxxing, studymaxxing, productivitymaxxing, cozymaxxing. Most non-looks formations are jocular. Double x is the usual spelling.

He's sleepmaxxing before the exam period starts.
My feed is suddenly full of studymaxxing and productivitymaxxing tips.

138. X-farming / farm X

Indicates deliberate behavior intended to accumulate, maximize, or generate a valued resource, metric, social reaction, or impression. Borrowed from gaming, where farming is repetitive resource acquisition. Occurs as a compound noun, as a verb with an object, and hyphenated.

The lexicalized instances the tagger should recognize:

  aura farming — behaving or presenting oneself so as to increase perceived
  coolness, charisma, mystique, or social presence.
    He's just standing there in sunglasses farming aura.
    That slow-motion entrance was pure aura farming.
    He spent the whole video aura-farming.

  rage farming — repeatedly or strategically producing provocative material to
  generate anger and thereby obtain attention.
    That account has been rage-farming for months.
    He keeps posting ridiculous takes to farm outrage.

  engagement farming — using posts or interaction strategies primarily to
  generate likes, comments, reposts, or views.
    Posting obvious questions like that is engagement farming.
    The account farms engagement by posting deliberately polarizing polls.

  karma farming — producing or reposting content primarily to accumulate Reddit
  karma or upvotes.
    People accused the account of karma farming.
    He's farming karma with easy questions in popular subreddits.

--------
H. Algospeak

Words, abbreviations, altered spellings, euphemisms, or symbols used in
response to real or perceived automated moderation, demonetization, filtering,
search restrictions, or reduced algorithmic distribution.

This is not a grammatical class but a grouping by motivation, since these items
share one interpretive policy. They must be resolved from context, and their
use does not itself indicate approval, endorsement, or promotion of the
underlying topic. They frequently occur in reporting, survivor accounts,
educational content, warnings, criticism, or discussions intended to raise
awareness of sensitive topics. See also SA (25) and SH (26).

139. corn / 🌽

A context-dependent substitution for porn or pornography. The emoji is the more reliable cue; the spelled form is highly ambiguous and needs supporting context.

His account got banned because he posted too much explicit corn.
Are there any good websites to watch free 🌽 on?

Compare: We bought corn at the market. → ordinary meaning.

140. grape / graped / 🍇

A context-dependent substitution for rape or raped, used especially when discussing sexual violence while attempting to avoid explicit terminology or perceived platform restrictions. Passive participle use with a human subject is close to decisive.

She said she was graped and later reported the assault.
More cases of women being graped in that area.

Compare: She bought grapes at the supermarket. → ordinary meaning.

141. seggs

An altered spelling of sex used especially in digital contexts. Unlike the other items in this section it is not a homograph of an ordinary word, so it is unambiguous when it occurs. Also written segs.

"Her post about relationships got a lot of comments talking about seggs.
They were joking in the group chat about having seggs.

142. unalive

A euphemistic verb used instead of kill or die. Unalived may also be used adjectivally in the sense of dead, and constructions such as unalive oneself may euphemistically refer to suicide. The most established item in this section, now also used self-consciously outside any moderation context.

The caption says the character was unalived off-screen.
He unalived himself last night.
